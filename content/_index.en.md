      text: |-
        My central question is: **as the diagnosis and treatment of childhood diseases increasingly depend on molecular evidence, how can a clinical-track student turn frontier literature into bedside-usable understanding through reading and computation?**

        I read and practice along three directions: genomic diagnosis for acutely ill newborns and children (the clinical translation path of rapid whole-genome sequencing); single-cell and spatial atlases reshaping how we see childhood disease (how to read and appraise this evidence); and the real boundaries of AI entering medicine (molecular structure prediction, medical language models).

        I treat literature reading as a trainable skill rather than passive information intake: a fixed reading framework, checked statistics, a clear separation between "what the authors claim" and "what the data support" — and, wherever possible, reproducing key figures as analyses I can rerun myself.
